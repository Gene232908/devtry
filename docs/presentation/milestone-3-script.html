Milestone 3 — Presentation Script

CarVibeDubai Admin Dashboard · read-aloud script with scenarios and coaching notes

How to use this script

SAY — read this out. Written to be spoken, not recited.
SCENARIO — the concrete example that makes the point land. Say it right after the explanation.
COACHING — for you only. Never read aloud.
IF ASKED — answers to likely questions. Do not volunteer these.
Slide 1

Opening

Say

Good day. Today I'll walk you through Milestone 3 — the admin dashboard for CarVibeDubai. This is the private side of the system: where you see every customer who requested a valuation, every car you've bought, and the commission owed each month. We'll go through eight screens, and I'll take questions at the end.

Coaching

Slow, confident pace. This slide just sets expectations — eight screens, questions at the end — so Sir Nyash knows the shape of the talk before it starts.

Slide 2

What Was Built in Milestone 3

Say

Milestone 1 was the public website — where the customer submits. Milestone 3 is the other end: what happens to that information after it's sent. Three things were added. First, the full lead pipeline, from "New" to "Deal." Second, tracking for cars you've purchased, plus the 2% commission on every sale. Third, spam protection — because every verification message we send is billed.

Scenario

Think of it this way: before Milestone 3, a submitted form just sat there with no next step. Now, that same form moves through a visible pipeline — New, Contacted, Deal — and if the car gets bought, it shows up in Inventory with the commission already calculated.

Coaching

The line "every message we send is billed" is the seed for the spam section later. Say it plainly here, no need to elaborate yet.

Slide 3

Dashboard — Overview

Scenario

Open /admin. Point to the eight pipeline cards, then the two charts.

Say

This is the first thing you see after signing in. At the top, the whole business at a glance — how many new leads, how many being worked, how many closed as deals. Below that, two charts: website visitors, and how many of them actually submitted a form. It updates on its own — no need to refresh.

Scenario

Say a customer submits a form right now, from their phone, while you're looking at this screen. Within seconds, the "New" card ticks up by one — you don't need to refresh the page or check another tab. That's the "updates on its own" part.

Coaching

Don't read every card. Point to two or three. If asked about a term, answer it — but don't pre-explain all eight, it drags.

If asked

"Why is there an info icon?" — The explanation is tucked behind that small icon to keep the screen clean. Just hover over it.

Slide 4

Leads — The Heart of the System

Scenario

Open Leads. Click into one lead to show detail and the status dropdown.

Say

This is every customer who completed the form. You'll see their name, the car, the mileage, and which Emirate they're in. You can search, filter, and export to Excel. When you open a lead, this is where you move it through the process — from "New," to "Contacted," to "Deal" once you've bought the car.

Scenario

Picture this: a customer calls you back after you contacted them, and now wants to sell. You open their lead, change the status from "Contacted" to "Deal," fill in the purchase price — and that's it. The car now appears automatically in Inventory, ready to be marked sold later.

Coaching

Spend the most time here — this is what gets used daily. Actually change a status live, in front of him. Seeing it move is what convinces, not the explanation.

Slide 5

Incomplete Forms — Recovering Lost Enquiries

Scenario

Open Incomplete Forms.

Say

This is one of the most valuable parts, so I want to explain it carefully. When a customer types their name and phone number but doesn't finish the form — maybe they got distracted, or something came up — that lead used to be gone completely. Now, the moment they enter a phone number, it's captured here. Think of this as a temporary holding area for people who showed interest but didn't complete the process.

Scenario

Here's exactly how it plays out: a customer opens the valuation form on their phone, types "Ahmed" and a WhatsApp number, then gets a call and closes the tab before finishing. That name and number are already sitting on this screen. You can call Ahmed directly, ask about his car over the phone, and record the deal manually — a lead that would have been lost is now a lead you can act on.

Say

And once they do finish the form, this entry disappears from here automatically and moves to Leads. They're never duplicated — one person, one place.

Scenario

Following the same example — if Ahmed comes back later and completes the form properly, his entry vanishes from Incomplete Forms and appears in Leads instead. You will never see "Ahmed" listed in both places at once.

Coaching

This is the part the owner will care about most — it's recovered revenue. Stress the line "you can call them directly." Pause briefly after it.

If asked

"What if they come back and finish it?" — It moves to Leads and disappears from here. It won't show in both places.

"Is the record lost when it moves?" — No, we keep the history so there's a trace of when they first started.

Slide 6

Phone Verification — and Why It Costs Money

Say

Before I go further, let me explain how a phone number gets verified. When a customer submits, we send a six-digit code to their WhatsApp. They have to type it back in to prove the number is real. That's how we guarantee every lead in your list has a genuine, reachable number — not something typed in randomly.

Say

But there's a cost to this: every single message we send is billed. So if someone requests a code over and over — on purpose or not — that cost falls on the business. That's exactly why four layers of protection exist.

Scenario

Without any limit, someone could sit there and hit "send code" fifty times in a row, or point the form at a random stranger's number just to be annoying — and every one of those fifty messages would be a real charge to you, even though not a single one leads to a customer.

Coaching

Don't skip this even though it sounds technical. Once he understands "it costs money," the next two slides become value, not complexity. Slow and clear.

Slide 7

Spam Protection — Four Limits

Say

Here's how the four protections work. First, one phone number can only request a code five times a day. Second, one internet connection can only request ten times a day, even if they try different numbers. Third, there's a system-wide daily cap of three hundred, so no matter what happens, your total cost has a ceiling. And fourth — this one's the smartest.

Say

If a number requests a code three times in a row and never successfully verifies, it's automatically stopped. Why? Because if that number really belongs to the customer, they'd receive the code and be able to type it in. Three failed attempts with zero successful verifications means that number almost certainly isn't theirs.

ProtectionLimitWhat it stops
Per number5 / dayThe same person requesting repeatedly
Per connection10 / dayOne person trying many different numbers
System-wide300 / dayA hard ceiling on total cost
Unverified streak3 in a rowA number that isn't actually theirs
Scenario

Imagine someone typing in a random UAE number that isn't theirs, just to see what happens. They can request a code up to three times — but since they never receive it themselves, they can never type it back in. On the third failed attempt, that number is automatically locked out. No manual work from you, and no fourth message gets billed.

Coaching

Spend the most time on the fourth rule — it's the clever one and the hardest to grasp at first. Use the logic: "if it's your number, you'll receive the code." That line explains itself.

If asked

"What if a real customer gets caught by mistake?" — They're not lost. They'll still show up on the Blocked Numbers screen with their number, so you can call them directly.

Slide 8

Blocked Numbers — Who Was Stopped, and Why

Scenario

Open Blocked Numbers. Point to the two tables.

Say

This is where every case of abuse shows up. There are two tables. The top one shows numbers that hit a limit in the last twenty-four hours — including how many times they tried, and which protection stopped them. The bottom one shows numbers that are permanently blocked.

Say

A number can land here two ways: automatically, when it hits one of the four limits — or manually, by you. If you spot a lead that's obviously fake, there's a "Mark as spam" button right there, and that number can never send another request. And if you ever make a mistake, there's an "Unblock" button to bring them back.

Scenario

Say you're reviewing Leads and see one entry with a clearly fake name and a number that never verified — you click "Mark as spam" right from that lead, and it's instantly added to Blocked Numbers. If it later turns out to be a real customer who mistyped something, you open Blocked Numbers and click "Unblock" — takes seconds, fully reversible.

Coaching

Show the "Mark as spam" and "Unblock" buttons live. The point: you're in control, not the machine making a final call. The fact that it's reversible is what removes the fear.

If asked

"Can I still see who they are?" — Yes. Their number and name stay listed, so if it turns out to be a real customer, you can still reach them.

Slide 9

Inventory — Cars You've Purchased

Scenario

Open Inventory. Point to the month filter and "Mark sold."

Say

When you mark a lead as "Deal," the car appears here automatically. There's no "add a car" button on purpose — every car in this list traces back to a real customer and a real purchase price. Once you sell it, you click "Mark sold," enter the sale price, and the profit and commission are calculated right there.

Scenario

Say you bought a Nissan Patrol for 40,000 AED from a lead. It's now sitting in Inventory as "in stock." Three weeks later you resell it for 50,000. You click "Mark sold," type in 50,000 — instantly, the system shows a 10,000 AED profit and a 200 AED commission, without you touching a calculator.

Coaching

"No add a car button" is by design, not a limitation. If asked, the reasoning is: every car should trace back to a real transaction. Allowing manual entries would break that trail.

Slide 10

Commission — The Monthly Share

Scenario

Open Commission.

Say

This is the 2% of profit earned on every car sold, grouped by month. It's calculated automatically — no manual bookkeeping. When you're ready to settle a month, you mark it "Issued," then "Paid." Once that happens, the figures are locked and can't be changed — so there's never any doubt later about what was actually paid.

Scenario

By the end of August, say five cars were sold with a combined profit of 50,000 AED. This page already shows August's commission as 1,000 AED — you didn't add a single number yourself. You mark it "Paid" once settled, and even if a sixth car from August turns up later, it rolls into September instead of quietly changing August's number.

Coaching

This is money, so use the word "locked." That's the reassurance he wants to hear — that recorded payments can't casually be changed.

Slide 11

Vehicle Catalog and Settings

Scenario

Open Vehicle Catalog, then Settings. Keep this brief.

Say

Vehicle Catalog controls which cars a customer can select on the public form. Add a new make or model here, and it appears on the website immediately — no need to contact us for that. Settings holds your WhatsApp number, your call number, and the email that gets notified when a new lead comes in.

Scenario

If you start dealing in a new brand — say, McLaren — you add it here yourself, and within seconds the very next customer opening the valuation form can select it. No waiting on us to push an update.

Coaching

Move fast through these two — they're supporting screens. The one line worth emphasizing is "no need to contact us" — that's the independence he's getting.

Slide 12

Sign-In Security

Say

Last technical point. This dashboard is for you alone. If someone tries to guess a password, only five wrong attempts are allowed before that account is temporarily locked out. We also never say whether the email or the password was wrong — both give the same message, so nobody guessing gets any useful information back.

Scenario

If someone got hold of your email and tried guessing passwords, they'd get five tries before being blocked for fifteen minutes — nowhere near enough to brute-force anything. Meanwhile, if you sign in with the correct password right after a couple of typos, it works immediately and the failed count resets to zero.

Coaching

Keep this short. If asked whether he could lock himself out — no, because the moment the correct password is entered, the counter clears.

Slide 13

Closing

Say

That covers Milestone 3. In short: you can now see every customer from the moment they first engage, all the way through to a completed purchase. Enquiries that would have been lost are now recoverable. And your messaging costs are protected automatically. I'll take any questions now.

Coaching

Stop talking after this. Let the silence sit — don't fill it. If there are no questions, prompt him: "Which part would you like to go back to?"
